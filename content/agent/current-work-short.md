Ilak's main project right now is Nature Foundation Models — a research program for building AI that learns from interacting with the world, not just from reading static data.

The idea is simple: watch what happens when you take an action, log that experience, and build understanding from it. The same pattern applies whether you're talking about a robot moving through a space, an agent in a puzzle environment, or a biological system you're trying to understand through experiments.

Inside that program she built ASRA — an open-source stack that runs the full loop: logging what happened, figuring out what actions mean, exploring on purpose, testing whether things generalize, and planning what to try next. She's also extended that thinking into decision biology — treating cells and pathways like systems you learn by intervening, not just observing.

She cares a lot about how you measure this kind of intelligence. She designed a benchmark called ASDB that asks: can an agent learn what tools do and what's really going on underneath, without cheating on the test? Her line is — figure out what to measure before you scale training.

On the side she's competing in NeuroGolf on Kaggle, building tiny circuits that solve ARC-style puzzles and checking that they actually generalize, not just score well on a leaderboard.

The through-line is interaction-grounded reasoning: test under real conditions, keep experiment logs instead of vague summaries, and refuse to claim something works until held-out evidence says so.
