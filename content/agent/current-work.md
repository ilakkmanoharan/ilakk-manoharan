Ilak's current project is **Nature Foundation Models (NFM)**—a research program for building systems that learn representations, dynamics, causal structure, and mechanisms from interaction with natural systems, not from passive pattern matching alone.

She published the program on [SciLayer](https://sci-layer.vercel.app/authors/0009-0008-8073-5416). NFM is organized as a hierarchy: the top-level program; **NFM-Worlds** for learning how environments evolve through observation and intervention; **NFM-Robotics** for embodied interaction; **Atlas** as the robotics project family; and **Atlas-GS v1** as the first runnable implementation—RGB-D Gaussian mapping, persistent scene memory, transition logging, and **0.0102 m** localization RMSE on TUM `fr1_xyz` (4,018 Gaussians, CPU-first). The shared abstraction across domains is **State_t + Action_t → State_{t+1}**—the same loop whether the state is a robot scene, a grid world, or a biological pathway under perturbation. The NFM program repository is not open source today; the framework and implementations are documented in SciLayer preprints and the public program site.

Inside that program, [**ASRA**](https://sci-layer.vercel.app/articles/asra-integrated-architecture) (Adaptive Scientific Reasoning Architecture) is the nine-phase stack—and it is [open source](https://github.com/ilakkmanoharan/asra). ASRA operationalizes the interaction loop end to end: transition logging, semantics inferred from effects, directed exploration under budget, mechanism inference, planning, robustness evals, a **Decision Biology** bridge, and integration. Each phase ships inspectable artifacts—runnable modules and paired SciLayer manuscripts—not one brittle end-to-end policy. On **ARC-AGI-3**-style benchmarks, action labels are latent: the agent must infer what actions mean from transitions alone. Phase 1 logs every transition in a canonical schema; Phase 2 builds object-centric structure; Phase 3 adds directed exploration, episodic memory, and replay. She deployed that lineage into ARC Prize 2026 competition agents. The same stack extends to **Decision Biology**, where cells are probabilistic decision systems, perturbations are interventions, and pathways behave like latent world models—the operational question becomes which intervention to test next.

She recently argued in SciLayer that adaptive agents need **transition-centric memory** (experiment logs, not compressed observations) and **directed exploration** (novelty and usefulness decomposed under a step budget)—see [transition-centric memory and directed exploration](https://sci-layer.vercel.app/articles/transition-centric-memory-and-directed-exploration-asra). That distinction matters for any system whose behavior must be evaluated under intervention, not reconstructed from snapshots alone.

On measurement, she designed [**ASDB**](https://sci-layer.vercel.app/articles/adaptive-scientific-discovery-benchmark-asdb) (Adaptive Scientific Discovery Benchmark). Track A grades recovery of unlabeled action semantics from transitions; Track B grades hidden-mechanism inference and held-out prediction under an intervention budget. Combined A→B episodes score tool-learning and theory-learning separately, use fake competing hypotheses to prevent curve-fitting, and require full step-by-step logs for audit. The question she keeps asking across this work: **what should we measure before we pour data into training?** Reliability is a research problem—not a product checkbox. Define the construct, build the environment that enforces it, run generalization experiments, then scale.

In parallel, she is working on [**ARC-NeuroGolf**](https://github.com/ilakkmanoharan/ARC-NeuroGolf) for the **NeuroGolf 2026** Kaggle competition. NeuroGolf asks what is the cheapest ONNX circuit that implements an ARC transformation. The compiler synthesizes minimal graphs, scores them under minimum-description-length, and gates acceptance on **ARC-GEN** generalization—because leaderboard solve counts mislead without held-out verification.

Taken together, the integrated NFM + ASRA stack moves scientific AI from pattern matching to **interaction-grounded reasoning**—action semantics from transitions, exploration under budget, robustness evals before scaling features, and a path from grid-world reasoning to biological intervention semantics. By the numbers: 9 shipped ASRA phases; Atlas-GS v1 at 0.0102 m RMSE; a full SciLayer corpus documenting NFM → NFM-Worlds → NFM-Robotics → Atlas → Atlas-GS.
