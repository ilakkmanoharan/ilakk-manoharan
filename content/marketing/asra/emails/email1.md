I'm building **Nature Foundation Models** — a company focused on a problem most AI systems still get wrong: **scientific reasoning under uncertainty**.

Today, the best models in biology and science are extraordinarily good at **pattern prediction**. They are much weaker at what actual science requires: choosing interventions, learning causal structure, updating beliefs from experiments, and discovering hidden mechanisms in unfamiliar systems.

That gap is expensive. Drug discovery, target validation, and biological research still depend on slow, manual experimentation because our AI stack was built for static datasets—not for closed-loop scientific inquiry.

**What we're building**

At the center is **ASRA** (Adaptive Scientific Reasoning Architecture): a reasoning engine for world-model construction, causal inference, intervention learning, and adaptive experimentation.

Our first domain is **Decision Biology** — treating cells as adaptive decision systems that process signals, respond to perturbations, and evolve under uncertainty. We model:

- perturbations as **interventions**
- signaling pathways as **world models**
- cellular adaptation as **causal reasoning**

This is not another LLM wrapper or prediction pipeline. ASRA is designed to learn **what actions mean** from interaction itself—then use that understanding to propose, test, and revise scientific hypotheses.

Long term, Nature Foundation Models becomes a **multi-domain scientific intelligence platform** spanning biology, chemistry, physics, and environmental systems.

**Why now**

Three forces are converging:

1. **Foundation models proved** that large-scale representation learning works at scale.
2. **Biology is becoming an intervention-rich state space** — single-cell atlases, perturbation compendia (LINCS, scPerturb), and multimodal datasets make closed-loop reasoning possible for the first time.
3. **Scientific AI is shifting** from prediction toward reasoning, agents, and experimentation — but most systems still assume predefined action spaces and known environment semantics.

We believe the next major platform opportunity is not a bigger chat model. It is **infrastructure for machines that reason about nature the way scientists do**.
