# 150-word poster abstract

ASRA-NFM is a neuro-symbolic adaptive reasoning architecture that learns world dynamics through intervention rather than passive prediction. It builds object-centric structured states, records before–action–after transitions, maintains competing symbolic hypotheses about mechanisms, and selects actions that either advance an inferred goal or maximize information gain under uncertainty. Functional modules organize questioning (Tabby), evidence retrieval (Grubby), and decision-making (Sam) without requiring separate language models. The design targets interactive scientific reasoning—initially on ARC-AGI-style environments—with a shared core intended for autonomous experimentation, robotics, and physical AI. Evaluation compares random, heuristic, neural-only, symbolic-only, and hybrid baselines, with ablations on relations, hypothesis memory, neural world models, active experiments, and failure memory. Results fields remain placeholders until measured data are supplied; the central claim is stated as a hypothesis pending controlled validation.
